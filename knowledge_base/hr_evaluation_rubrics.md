# HR Evaluation Rubrics

Recruiters typically scan a resume for 6-10 seconds on the first pass, looking for job
titles, company names, dates, and a handful of standout keywords before deciding whether to
read further.

Relevance to the target role is weighted heavily: a candidate's most recent and most
prominent bullet points should align with the skills and responsibilities listed in the job
description.

Career progression matters. Recruiters look for increasing responsibility, scope, or
seniority over time rather than lateral or unclear moves, especially for mid-to-senior
candidates.

Gaps in employment are not automatically disqualifying, but unexplained gaps longer than six
months without context (education, freelancing, career break) can raise questions.

Consistency between the resume, LinkedIn profile, and any application materials is checked
during later screening stages; major discrepancies in dates or titles are a red flag.

For technical roles, evaluators weigh hands-on project or production experience with the
specific stack mentioned in the job posting more heavily than certifications alone.

Soft skills (communication, leadership, collaboration) are credible only when demonstrated
through specific examples (e.g. "Led a team of 4 engineers") rather than listed as standalone
traits.
