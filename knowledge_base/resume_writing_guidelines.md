# Resume Writing Guidelines

Start bullet points with strong action verbs ("Built", "Led", "Designed", "Optimized")
rather than passive phrases ("Was responsible for", "Helped with").

Quantify impact wherever possible. Prefer "Reduced page load time by 40%" over "Improved
page load time". Numbers make achievements concrete and memorable.

Each bullet point should follow a roughly STAR structure: the situation or task, the action
taken, and the measurable result, compressed into a single concise line.

Keep the summary section to 2-3 sentences focused on years of experience, core skills, and
the type of role being targeted. Avoid generic statements like "Hardworking team player
seeking opportunities".

Group skills into categories (e.g. "Languages", "Frameworks", "Tools") rather than listing
them as a flat, unsorted list. This improves scannability for recruiters.

Project descriptions should mention the problem solved, the technologies used, and the
outcome or scale (e.g. users served, performance improvement, or business impact).

Limit resumes to one page for early-career candidates and at most two pages for senior
candidates with extensive experience.

Remove outdated or irrelevant sections such as "References available upon request" or
objective statements that do not add value.

Proofread carefully: inconsistent verb tense (mixing past and present tense for current vs.
past roles), spelling errors, and inconsistent punctuation are common red flags for
recruiters.
